# Branch-volume diagnostics


1. Distinguish silhouette/volume defects from local faceting and display-normal artifacts. A normal change or smaller derivative error does not establish a better whole form.
2. Inspect guide coverage and endpoint behavior across the entire affected region. Local interpolation evidence does not validate extrapolation outside the guides.
3. Distinguish preserved vertices from preserved edges and triangle interiors. Inserting new points can change the actual surface while old vertices remain fixed.
4. Separate source/function requirements, protection of accepted regions, and algorithm-convenience constraints. Failure of one construction does not prove all fixed boundaries impossible.
5. When equivalent local changes only relocate a defect or create compensating lobes, stop that repair class and reconsider the representation. Preserve rejected and invalidated results with their different meanings.
6. Review comparable whole-form views and the complete longitudinal/transverse paths. Perform actual spatial intersection and inner-wall separation checks early on a visually improved coarse trial, before costly detailing.
7. Keep visual, topology, geometric contact, preservation, and downstream asset gates independent. Passing one gate does not stand in for another; selected samples do not prove an entire surface invariant.
